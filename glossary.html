Glossary

Key terms and concepts in pesticide poisoning surveillance and health-economic evaluation

Published

May 10, 2026

This glossary explains the technical terms used across the D2P Project — surveillance epidemiology, clinical toxicology, health economics, and South African public-health institutions. It is aimed at policymakers, clinicians, and students new to one or more of these fields.

Tip

Use the table of contents on the right (or the search icon, top right) to jump to a specific term. Terms are grouped by theme rather than alphabetised.

A. The disease problem

Pesticide poisoning

Acute or chronic illness caused by exposure to pesticide chemicals — most commonly organophosphates (OPs) and carbamates in South Africa, often through ingestion (intentional self-harm or accidental, particularly in children) or occupational exposure (farmworkers).

Organophosphate (OP)

A class of pesticide that inhibits the enzyme acetylcholinesterase, causing a build-up of the neurotransmitter acetylcholine. Clinically this produces the cholinergic toxidrome: salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, GI cramps, emesis (“SLUDGE”), bronchospasm, bradycardia, miosis, muscle fasciculations, weakness, and — in severe cases — respiratory failure, seizures, and coma. Examples seen in SA poisonings: terbufos, methamidophos, chlorpyrifos, methomyl (“aldicarb-style street pesticides”, often illegally repackaged).

Carbamate

A related class of cholinesterase inhibitors with usually shorter-duration enzyme inhibition than OPs. Treatment is similar (atropine) but pralidoxime is generally not indicated.

Cholinesterase / Butyrylcholinesterase (BChE)

The enzyme inhibited by OPs and carbamates. BChE (also called pseudocholinesterase or plasma cholinesterase) is measured in blood as a biomarker of exposure: a level <50% of the patient’s baseline (or <50% of the lab reference range) indicates significant inhibition. The NHLS BChE assay is the routine SA test used to detect pesticide poisoning.

Atropine

The first-line antidote for cholinergic toxicity — a competitive muscarinic-receptor antagonist. Given in escalating doses titrated to drying of secretions and resolution of bronchospasm.

Pralidoxime (PAM, 2-PAM)

An oxime that reactivates acetylcholinesterase if given before the enzyme-OP complex “ages”. Used in OP poisoning (not carbamate). Available in SA but underutilised.

OPIDN (Organophosphate-Induced Delayed Neuropathy)

A rare but serious long-term complication of OP poisoning: a peripheral neuropathy that emerges 1–3 weeks after exposure, causing weakness and paraesthesia. Estimated to occur in ~10% of severe OP survivors. Drives most of the long-term disability burden (YLD) in our model.

Intermediate syndrome

A neuromuscular weakness syndrome appearing 1–4 days after acute OP poisoning, distinct from initial cholinergic crisis and from OPIDN. Often requires prolonged ventilatory support.

B. Surveillance terms

Surveillance

The ongoing, systematic collection, analysis, and interpretation of health data — for action. The classic Langmuir definition emphasises “information for action”: data that doesn’t drive a decision is not surveillance.

Notifiable Medical Condition (NMC)

A disease that, by law (SA: National Health Act, 2003 and the NMC Regulations), must be reported by clinicians and laboratories to the National Department of Health. Pesticide poisoning is a Category 2 NMC in South Africa, meaning notification is required within 7 days. The NICD operates the NMC surveillance system.

Hotspot

A geographic and/or temporal cluster of cases above the expected background rate. Detection of hotspots — and timely field investigation — is a primary purpose of pesticide-poisoning surveillance. Drives the detection probability parameter in our decision model.

Sentinel surveillance

Surveillance that uses a selected, well-characterised network of reporting sites (e.g. tertiary hospitals) instead of attempting universal coverage. Trades population representativeness for higher data quality, completeness, and biological depth (e.g. specimen testing). Underpins our Option 6 (sentinel toxicology).

Case definition

The standardised criteria used to decide whether a patient counts as a “case” for surveillance. Distinguishes suspected, probable, and confirmed cases (the latter usually requiring laboratory confirmation).

Auto-notification

Automatic creation of a surveillance notification when a defined laboratory result is generated — without requiring a clinician to file a separate form. Our Option 3 proposes auto-notification when NHLS BChE drops below 50% of the reference range.

Vital Registration (VR)

The civil-registration system that records deaths and assigns an underlying cause using ICD-10 coding. SA’s VR system is operated by the Department of Home Affairs with cause-of-death coding by Stats SA. Known to under-code intentional self-poisoning, which is why our headline death estimate (1,500–1,800/year) sits above the VR-implied lower bound.

ICD-10

The 10th revision of the International Classification of Diseases — the standardised cause-of-death and cause-of-disease coding system. Pesticide-related ICD-10 codes (e.g. T60.0, X48, X68, Y18) are often miscoded as undetermined-intent or general-poisoning codes, biasing pesticide death counts downward.

C. Data sources used in the D2P Project

NICD

National Institute for Communicable Diseases. A division of the NHLS that operates SA’s national surveillance for communicable diseases and selected non-communicable conditions including pesticide poisoning. Hosts the NMC system.

NHLS

National Health Laboratory Service. The public-sector laboratory network covering ~80% of SA’s population. Source of routine BChE test results that can be used for laboratory-based surveillance (Option 1) and auto-notification (Option 3).

PIH (Poisons Information Helpline)

Red Cross War Memorial Children’s Hospital Poisons Information Helpline. A 24-hour clinical-toxicology advice line for clinicians and the public. Receives ~9,000 pesticide-related calls/year — a unique stream of data on mild and moderate cases that often never reach hospital.

DALRRD

Department of Agriculture, Land Reform and Rural Development. The SA regulator for pesticide registration and product control. Holds product-volume and active-ingredient data relevant to source attribution (Option 2).

EHP

Environmental Health Practitioner. Local-government public-health staff responsible for environmental investigations including pesticide-incident response. The principal field workforce activated under Option 5.

D. Burden estimation

Burden of disease

The total population-level impact of a condition, expressed in cases, deaths, DALYs (see below), or monetary terms. Our headline burden: ~11,690 cases, ~1,500–1,800 deaths, ~R2.3B/year.

Mutually-exclusive sum

The assumption that two or more data streams capture different cases with no overlap. Used here as the most defensible position when record linkage is not possible. Adding NMC + NHLS + PIH gives an upper bound that is robust if linkage later finds duplicates (one would simply subtract them).

Case Fatality Rate (CFR)

The proportion of cases that die. Stratified in our model by severity:

  • PIH-managed (mostly mild): 0.5%
  • NHLS-detected severe BChE inhibition: 30% (Buckley et al. 2021)
  • NMC-notified hospitalised cases: 15%

A flat 12.5% CFR applied to the total burden gives ~1,460 deaths; the stratified weighting gives ~830. The headline 1,500–1,800 sits in the upper-mid VR-triangulation range.

Years of Life Lost (YLL)

A mortality measure — the years between age at death and a reference life expectancy. SA pesticide deaths have a median age of ~30 and a reference e64 ≈ 64, giving ~30 YLL per death.

Years Lived with Disability (YLD)

A morbidity measure — duration of an illness × a disability weight (0–1). For pesticide poisoning, most survivors have minimal long-term YLD; the OPIDN sub-group drives the population YLD signal.

DALY (Disability-Adjusted Life-Year)

DALY = YLL + YLD. One DALY = one year of healthy life lost. The standard global metric for comparing burden across diseases.

E. Health economics

Direct medical costs

Costs incurred by the health system to treat the disease — admissions, drugs, investigations, ICU days. In SA public sector these are anchored to the UPFS (Uniform Patient Fee Schedule).

UPFS

Uniform Patient Fee Schedule — the SA National Department of Health tariff for public-sector services. Used as our cost reference: ward R1,800/day, high-care R5,500/day, ICU R13,500/day, ventilation surcharge +R4,000/day (2024).

Indirect costs

Productivity losses to the patient, family, and economy — lost wages, caregiver time, future earnings forgone for those who die or are disabled. Estimated here using the human-capital approach.

Human-capital approach

Values a death or disability at the discounted future earnings the person would have generated. We use 25 productive years × R60,000/year median income → ~R1.5M per death.

Value of a Statistical Life (VSL)

An alternative valuation based on revealed/stated preferences for marginal mortality-risk reduction. Typically ~3.3× annual income in LMIC settings. Gives a higher headline figure (~R5M/death) — useful as an advocacy ceiling.

Cost-effectiveness threshold (λ)

The maximum amount a payer will spend per DALY averted. SA’s empirical threshold (Edoka & Stacey, 2020) is ~R38,500/DALY. An intervention with cost-per-DALY below this is judged cost-effective.

ICER (Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio)

\[\text{ICER} = \frac{C_1 - C_0}{E_1 - E_0}\] The extra cost per extra unit of effect (e.g. per DALY averted, per case detected) when moving from a comparator to a new intervention. Compared against λ to decide cost-effectiveness.

Net Monetary Benefit (NMB)

\[\text{NMB} = \lambda \cdot \Delta E - \Delta C\] Equivalently for our framework: \(\text{NMB}_i = (C_0 - C_i) - K_i\), where \(C_i\) is the residual burden after intervention \(i\) and \(K_i\) is the intervention’s own cost. Positive NMB = good value. Our Full Package gives ~R260M/year NMB.

Return on Investment (ROI)

Avoided burden ÷ intervention cost. Headline: ~220–1,100× depending on whether costs are counted on the narrow surveillance perspective (R237k) or whole-system (R1.19M).

Avoided burden

The portion of the status-quo economic burden that is prevented by an intervention. In our model: \[C_{\text{avoided},i} = C_{\text{burden}} \cdot d_i \cdot \eta\] where \(d_i\) is the option’s incremental detection probability and \(\eta\) is the response efficacy (probability that an investigated hotspot leads to a successful intervention preventing further cases).

Response efficacy (\(\eta\))

The probability that, conditional on a hotspot being detected and investigated, the response actually averts further cases. Base case 0.30; sensitivity range 0.15–0.50.

Detection probability (\(d_i\))

The proportion of true hotspots that are detected and acted upon under option \(i\). Status quo ≈ 45%; Full Package ≈ 83%.

Induced demand / whole-system cost

The additional health-system activity generated by improved surveillance — more admissions reaching the surveillance bucket, more lab tests, more EHP investigations. The narrow Full Package cost (R237k) covers only surveillance coordination; the whole-system cost (R1.19M) adds the induced demand.

F. Decision-analytic methods

Decision tree

A model that maps out the probabilistic consequences of mutually-exclusive choices. Used here to compare Status Quo vs. Options 1–6 vs. Full Package in terms of cost and avoided burden. Built in Amua (an open-source decision-modelling tool).

Probabilistic Sensitivity Analysis (PSA) / Monte Carlo

Running the model thousands of times, each iteration drawing each parameter from its uncertainty distribution. Produces a distribution of NMBs rather than a point estimate.

Cost-Effectiveness Acceptability Curve (CEAC)

A plot showing, for each candidate strategy and at each value of \(\lambda\), the probability that the strategy is the most cost-effective. The strategy with the highest curve at the SA threshold (R38,500/DALY) is preferred.

Tornado diagram

A horizontal-bar plot showing how much the headline result moves when each parameter is varied across its plausible range, holding others fixed. Identifies the parameters that drive the result.

G. Policy options in this project

Option 1 — NHLS data MOU

A monthly aggregated data feed from NHLS to NICD/NMC for severe BChE-inhibition cases.

Option 2 — DALRRD product linkage

Cross-reference clusters of cases against DALRRD pesticide product-volume data to attribute likely active ingredient and source.

Option 3 — BChE auto-notification

NHLS LIS automatically generates an NMC notification within 24 hours of a severe BChE result. Highest individual-option detection gain in the model.

Option 4 — PIH quarterly reports

Structured quarterly clinical-toxicology summaries from PIH integrated into NMC review.

Option 5 — Provincial response activation

Standardised protocol for rapid field investigation of detected hotspots. Triggered by Options 3 or 4.

Option 6 — Sentinel toxicology

A small network of tertiary hospitals (CHB → Groote Schuur, Albert Luthuli, Universitas, Steve Biko) systematically collecting biological specimens for GC-MS / LC-MS chemical attribution in living patients. Enables active product-level intelligence (which active ingredient, which formulation) that the other options cannot provide.

Full Package

All of Options 1–5 implemented together. Detection ≈ 83%. Annual cost: R237k narrow / R1.19M whole-system. Option 6 is treated as a parallel research investment, not part of the operational package.

H. Statistical & data terminology

Sample size

The number of observations needed to estimate a parameter with a given precision. For Option 6 sentinel toxicology, we use \(n = 323\) per site for \(p = 0.30\), \(d = 0.05\), \(\alpha = 0.05\).

Confidence interval (CI)

A range that, on repeated sampling, would contain the true parameter the stated proportion of the time (e.g. 95%). Wider CIs imply less precise estimates.

Distribution

A description of how a parameter varies. In PSA we use beta distributions for proportions (CFR, response efficacy), gamma for costs, and fixed for deterministic structural assumptions.

Triangulation

Cross-checking a finding using independent data sources or methods. The pesticide death estimate is triangulated across NMC × CFR-derived deaths × Vital Registration ICD-10-coded deaths.

I. Tools & software

Quarto

The publication system used to build this website. Combines code (R, Python, Julia), prose, equations, and interactive elements into static HTML, PDF, or Word output.

Amua

An open-source decision-modelling tool used to build the decision tree, run PSA, and export results. The CSV at amua_import_parameters_v3.csv is the parameter-import file.

R / tidyverse

The statistical-computing environment and the package ecosystem used for data wrangling, tables, and figures across the analysis.

GC-MS / LC-MS

Gas / Liquid Chromatography – Mass Spectrometry — laboratory techniques for identifying and quantifying specific chemicals in biological or environmental samples. The technical foundation of Option 6 sentinel toxicology.
