Policy Brief 1: Strengthening Pesticide Poisoning Surveillance

Closing the detection gap between NMC, NHLS, and PIH

ImportantFor Decision-Makers

Target audience: Ministerial Advisory Committee (MAC), National Department of Health (NDoH), Provincial Health Leadership, NICD/NHLS leadership.

Companion briefs: Coordination · Terbufos restriction.

1. Policy question

Should NDoH invest in automated, multi-source pesticide-poisoning surveillance to close the detection gap between the NMC, NHLS laboratory, and PIH data streams?

2. Public health problem

South Africa under-detects pesticide poisoning. Three data streams each see a fraction of the problem; none sees the whole:

  • Most cases never appear in NMC.
  • Severe laboratory results do not trigger a public-health alert.
  • The country’s largest single source of clinical signal — the Poisons Information Helpline — is not used for surveillance at all.

Hotspots are detected late or not at all; clusters from contaminated food, illegal sales, or unsafe occupational exposure run their course before any agency responds; and the country has no real-time view of which products are causing harm.

NoteHealth-sector share of the burden — what surveillance unlocks

The public health system bears ~R80M of the R2.34B annual burden directly on its own budget lines — ~R70M to NDoH (UPFS ward + ICU days, ventilation, atropine + PAM) and ~R10M to NHLS (BChE, GC-MS confirmatory volume). Every percentage point of additional detection (Options 1–5 below) reduces ward-days, ICU-days, and repeat lab workload before any mortality benefit is counted — so the surveillance investment pays back on the NDoH and NHLS budget lines first, and on household / societal mortality (~R2.25B) second.

Architecturally, the three streams are unlinked:

What this means in plain numbers. NMC sees ~690 cases/year. NHLS sees ~2,000 severe BChE results/year on live patients but does not auto-notify. PIH receives ~9,000 calls/year that are never aggregated to a surveillance dataset. The true annual burden of ~11,690 cases is therefore observed at < 6 % completeness by NMC alone.

Detection vs. true burden — a critical distinction

Better surveillance has two opposite numerical effects that decision-makers must not confuse:

Effect Direction Driver Interpretation
Observed burden ↑ Better case-finding (auto-notify, MVD ingest) The headline “case count” will rise as detection improves — this is success, not failure.
True burden ↓ Faster source removal, targeted regulation, prevention Number of actual poisonings falls as hotspots are addressed earlier.

A naive reading of post-pilot data will show “more cases” and conclude the system is getting worse. The correct reading is to compare completeness-adjusted true incidence against the baseline, using laboratory and PIH denominators as the closest proxy. This needs to be agreed up front with NDoH and the MAC.

4. Root cause analysis

The four root causes are all executional, not legislative. Each one is already legally permissible under Section 90 of the National Health Act (2003) and the existing NMC regulations.

5. Policy options

Option S1 — Activate the NHLS→NMC IT feed (MOU exists)

The NHLS–NICD MOU for routine BChE data sharing already exists on paper. What is missing is the IT implementation — a working LIS-to-NMC connector (HL7 / structured flat-file export), QA governance, and a monthly reconciliation. Detection rises from ~4.5 to ~5.2 hotspots / 10 (annual cost ~R40k ongoing + ~R80k one-off, 8–12 weeks).

Option S3a — BChE auto-notification (★ recommended — capability exists)

Configure the NHLS LIS to fire an automatic NICD notification when a BChE result shows severe inhibition (< 50 % of normal), within 24 hours of validation. No new test, no new reagent contract — only an IT configuration on top of the ~9,000 BChE tests/year already performed. Detection rises to ~6.7 / 10 for R55k/year in 2–4 weeks. This is the highest-value, fastest option on the menu.

Option S3b — Live-patient chemical toxicology + auto-notification (★ recommended, medium term)

NHLS currently performs chemical toxicology (qualitative/quantitative pesticide assays) post-mortem only. There is no live-patient toxicology capability. Option S3b funds:

  • Phase 1: a sentinel tertiary site (e.g. CHB) to perform live-patient chemical toxicology.
  • Phase 2–3: 4–5 site rollout with auto-notification wired in.

The clinical-surveillance value is agent attribution: S3a tells us a poisoning happened; S3b tells us which product. Without S3b, ~10,000 survivors/year are recorded with agent “unknown / suspected”, which makes targeted regulation (see Terbufos brief) impossible. Cost: ~R1.2M pilot site → ~R3.6M for 5-site rollout, 6–12 months. Funded from research-surveillance streams (SAMRC, Bloomberg, Gates), not the surveillance operating budget.

Option S4 — PIH live dashboard + Minimum Viable Dataset (★ recommended)

Two layers:

  • Short term (1–2 weeks): PIH publishes a live dashboard of pesticide-related calls (counts, geography, agent, severity, demographics). No new data; just a daily extract from existing call records.
  • Medium term (3–9 months): PIH and NICD agree a Minimum Viable Dataset (MVD) so the ~9,000 calls/year can be ingested into NMC as a structured surveillance stream.

Detection rises to ~6.1 / 10 for R62k/year. The dashboard is the lowest-hanging fruit in the whole portfolio: it ships in days.

6. Decision tree — from surveillance investment to averted burden

Cases averted = (P_detect − 0.45) × η × 11,690. Deaths averted = cases averted × p_death (0.13). The MVP package averts ≈ 150 deaths/year for ≈ R157,000 spend — ~R1,000 per death averted against a per-death societal cost of R1.5M.

7. Implementation considerations

Component Lead agency Year-1 cost Critical path
S1 IT feed NHLS IT + NICD middleware R40k + R80k one-off LIS-to-NMC HL7 connector spec
S3a BChE auto-notify NHLS LIS team R55k LIS rule configuration; QA review
S3b Live toxicology NHLS clinical chemistry + sentinel hospital R1.2M pilot Lab method validation; sample logistics
S4 PIH dashboard PIH (Tygerberg) + NICD R62k Daily extract + hosting; MVD workshop
Hotspot detection (SaTScan) NICD epi analyst R40k Geocoding of NMC + MVD ingest

Privacy & legal. All flows are de-identified (age, sex, facility, result, date). POPIA-compliant under existing Section 90 NHA authority. No new legislation required.

Risks. (i) Low BChE testing uptake by clinicians — S3a fires only on tests ordered. (ii) Data-quality drift if facility metadata is incomplete (geocoding will fail). (iii) Capacity bottleneck at provincial response level — the Coordination brief addresses this.

8. Recommended next step

Adopt the Surveillance MVP (S1 + S3a + S4) and pilot in 3 provinces (Gauteng, Western Cape, KZN) for 6 months. Approximate pilot budget: ~R157,000 operating + ~R80,000 IT setup. Lead agency: NICD as national coordinator.

  • Weeks 1–2: S4 dashboard live; S3a LIS configuration begins.
  • Months 1–3: S1 IT connector built and tested; S4 MVD workshop with PIH.
  • Month 6: Evaluate against (a) completeness of NHLS severe results in NMC and (b) cluster-detection latency.
  • Month 9: Add S3b sentinel toxicology site, funded from a separate research-surveillance stream.

The coordination side of the same system — how NICD, EHPs, DALRRD, and provincial response teams act on the improved signal — is covered in the Coordination brief. The product-specific regulatory action that S3b makes possible is the subject of the Terbufos brief.
