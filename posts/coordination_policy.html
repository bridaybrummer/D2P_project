Policy Brief 2: Closing the Coordination Loop

Making NMC, EHPs, DALRRD, and provincial response work as one system

ImportantFor Decision-Makers

Target audience: Ministerial Advisory Committee (MAC), National Department of Health (NDoH), DALRRD Pesticide Registrar, Provincial Health Leadership.

Companion briefs: Surveillance · Terbufos restriction.

1. Policy question

Should NDoH and DALRRD mandate a closed-loop coordination workflow — where every NMC notification triggers an investigation that is recorded on NMC, every EHP→DALRRD referral is structured, and every DALRRD action is fed back to NICD and the clinician?

2. Public health problem

Better detection (see the Surveillance brief) is wasted if the system does not act on what it detects. South Africa’s pesticide-poisoning response chain currently has eight sequential decision points between “case suspected” and “regulatory action closes the loop”, and six of those eight default to a broken-loop terminal. The result is that a case which is correctly notified can still produce no investigation, no source removal, no regulatory follow-up, and no feedback to the notifier — leaving the underlying hazard in place and producing predictable secondary cases.

The notification pathway — one-way and broken

Reading the diagram. Clinicians do file on NMC — that is the formal entry point. NMC does SMS the local EHP, who is mandated to investigate. The chain breaks at two specific places: (a) the EHP investigation outcome is never written back to NMC because no NMC field requires it; and (b) the EHP→DALRRD referral, when it happens, is informal and likewise unrecorded. DALRRD action — if any — flows back to the EHP at best, and never to NICD or the originating clinician.

4. Root cause analysis

The five drivers are: (1) NMC schema lacks a closure-of-loop field; (2) EHP→DALRRD referral has no SOP; (3) DALRRD has no inbound NICD intelligence feed despite a formal request for one; (4) clinicians get no case-status feedback so the notification has no perceived value; (5) no decision node in the chain has an accountable owner with a KPI, so nothing fails loudly when the loop breaks.

5. Policy options

Option C1 — Mandate an NMC closure-of-loop field for EHPs

Add a structured “investigation outcome” field to NMC against every notification: visited/not visited, source identified/not, environmental sampling done/not, referral made/not. Make completion within 14 days an EHP performance KPI. No new legislation; an NMC schema change and circular from NDoH. Annual cost: ~R30,000 (schema update + training material).

Option C2 — Structured EHP→DALRRD referral form on NMC

Add a referral form to NMC that auto-emails the DALRRD Pesticide Registrar/Inspectorate when an EHP suspects an unsafe product, illegal sale, or occupational source. Captures product, location, suspected agent, severity, and EHP contact. Replaces today’s informal phone/email referral. Annual cost: ~R25,000.

Option C3 — NICD→DALRRD poisoning intelligence feed (★ recommended)

DALRRD has formally requested poisoning intelligence to inform registration, labelling, and inspectorate priorities. Output: a quarterly NICD brief by suspected product / geography / season, plus an ad-hoc cluster-alert channel. Closes the regulator-feedback gap and gives DALRRD the evidence base for product-specific action (the explicit prerequisite for the Terbufos brief recommendation). Annual cost: ~R40,000 (NICD analyst + DALRRD liaison), 12 weeks.

Option C4 — Auto-feedback to notifying clinician

NMC sends the notifying clinician a quarterly case-status summary: outcome of EHP investigation, regulatory action taken, whether their case contributed to a detected cluster. Removes the “I notify into a black hole” disincentive. Annual cost: ~R20,000 (templating + email integration).

Option C5 — Joint KPI dashboard

A small public dashboard tracking: median time from notification to EHP investigation; % of notifications with a recorded outcome; % of clusters with documented DALRRD action; % of notifying clinicians who received feedback. Co-owned by NICD, DALRRD, and provincial DoHs. Annual cost: ~R30,000.

Coordination Full Package (C1 + C2 + C3 + C4 + C5)

Total annual cost of the Coordination Full Package: ~R145,000. No new legislation required.

Institutional mechanism. A quarterly Joint Coordination Forum (NICD chair; DALRRD, NHLS, PIH, and one rotating provincial DoH representative) reviews the KPI dashboard, signs off cluster-alert escalations, and resolves cross-departmental blockers. Mandated by joint NDoH–DALRRD Director-General circular. No new legislation required.

Risks and mitigations.

  • EHP non-compliance with new NMC field. Mitigation: 14-day completion KPI tied to provincial performance reviews; circular from NDoH DG.
  • DALRRD capacity to act on referrals. Mitigation: C3 quarterly brief lets DALRRD prioritise by burden rather than respond reactively.
  • Privacy concerns on clinician auto-feedback. Mitigation: feedback is aggregate at clinician level, not patient-identifying.

8. Recommended next step

Adopt C1 + C3 as the Coordination MVP, and the Full Package within 12 months.

  • Weeks 1–4: NDoH–DALRRD joint DG circular committing to the closed-loop model; standing up the Joint Coordination Forum.
  • Weeks 4–12: C1 NMC schema change + EHP training; C3 NICD→DALRRD quarterly brief begins on the existing NMC data.
  • Months 3–12: C2 referral form, C4 auto-feedback, C5 KPI dashboard go live.
  • Month 12: Evaluate against (i) median notification-to-investigation time, (ii) % of notifications with recorded outcome, (iii) DALRRD-reported regulatory actions per quarter.
