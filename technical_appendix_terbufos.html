Technical Appendix C — Terbufos Bracketed Deaths-Averted Estimate

Closed-form attributable-fraction model for the T2 restriction option

Author

D2P Project

Published

May 15, 2026

NoteWhere this appendix fits

This appendix is the formal quantitative basis for the bracketed 200–400 deaths-averted-per-year estimate cited in the Terbufos brief. It is deliberately a closed-form attributable-fraction calculation — not a decision tree — because the upstream uncertainty is dominated by the attributable fraction itself.

Sister appendices: A — Surveillance decision analysis · B — Coordination model · D — Sentinel toxicology.

1 1. The formula

Annual deaths averted by removing terbufos from informal channels:

\[ \widehat{D}_{T2} \;=\; f_\text{attr} \times \varphi_\text{retired} \times N_\text{deaths} \]

where

  • \(f_\text{attr}\) = terbufos attributable fraction of fatal SA pesticide poisonings (CSV: frac_terbufos_attributable_{lo,mid,hi} = 0.2, 0.25, 0.35)
  • \(\varphi_\text{retired}\) = fraction of terbufos exposure successfully retired by enforcement (substitution-adjusted; placeholder 0.6 working, 0.4–0.8 sensitivity)
  • \(N_\text{deaths}\) = headline annual deaths (CSV: n_deaths_headline = 2,620)

2 2. Bracketed estimate

Working value: \(\widehat{D}_{T2}^{\text{mid}} = 0.25 \times 0.6 \times 2620 = 393\) deaths/yr.

Honest bracket: 210 (lower) — 734 (upper).

3 3. Cost side

4 4. Cost per death averted

Working value: cost/death-averted = R5,089.

This is well below any reasonable willingness-to-pay anchor for SA (e.g. 1× GDP/capita per DALY ≈ R85,000–R130,000), so T2 dominates “do nothing” even at the lower bound of deaths averted.

5 5. International anchors

  • Sri Lanka: Single-agent withdrawals of class I OPs (1995–2011) reduced national suicide rate by ~50% with no compensatory substitution to other methods (Gunnell et al. 2017).
  • Bangladesh: Similar withdrawal pattern, similar mortality reduction.
  • South Africa: Davies et al. 2023 — terbufos dominates paediatric fatal pesticide poisonings in Soweto.

The Sri Lanka and Bangladesh natural experiments are why we treat \(f_\text{attr} \in [0.20, 0.35]\) as defensible without a new SA-specific autopsy study.

6 6. Maize yield impact under a terbufos ban

This section underpins the brief paragraph in §7 of the Terbufos brief. The headline conclusion — <5 % yield impact, concentrated in non-Bt smallholder maize — rests on three multiplicative factors.

6.1 6.1 Closed form

\[ \Delta Y \;=\; s_{\text{non-Bt}} \times u_{\text{terbufos}} \times \delta_{\text{substitute}} \]

where

  • \(s_{\text{non-Bt}}\) = share of SA maize area not under Bt-trait varieties (~15 %; the Bt trait covers the dominant stalk-borer use case biologically).
  • \(u_{\text{terbufos}}\) = share of that non-Bt area where terbufos is the current soil-insect / nematode treatment (≤50 %; the rest already uses substitutes or no treatment).
  • \(\delta_{\text{substitute}}\) = residual per-hectare yield gap when terbufos is replaced by the leading registered substitute (chlorantraniliprole, thiamethoxam, imidacloprid, or biological control). International field-trial evidence: 0–10 %, working value ~5 %.

Working value: \(\Delta Y \approx 0.15 \times 0.50 \times 0.05 = 0.375 \%\) of national maize output. Plausible upper bound at \(s=0.20\), \(u=0.70\), \(\delta=0.10\): \(\Delta Y \approx 1.4 \%\). The brief’s “<5 %” headline is comfortably conservative.

6.2 6.2 Why the cost is bounded

  1. Bt-trait penetration. SA commercial maize is ~85 % Bt — the dominant stalk-borer use case is retired biologically before any chemical intervention. This is the single largest reason the SA yield exposure is small relative to the equivalent EU 2009 case.
  2. Substitute acute-hazard profile. Substitutes are 100–1,000× less acutely toxic on rat oral LD50 (chlorantraniliprole U, thiamethoxam II, imidacloprid II vs terbufos Ia). The acute-poisoning burden retired by withdrawal is therefore the full terbufos attributable share, not a redistribution to a similar-hazard substitute.
  3. EU 2009 natural experiment. Terbufos was withdrawn in the EU more than 15 years ago. No documented maize-yield collapse in any producer state. SA’s exposure is smaller than the EU’s was because of Bt penetration.

6.3 6.3 Input-cost delta

Substitutes cost roughly 2–5× per hectare vs commodity granular OPs. Maize gross output is ~R20,000/ha. The substitute-cost delta is therefore single-digit-% of gross output — meaningful for smallholder margins but not for the national maize balance sheet.

6.4 6.4 Smallholder transition support

The remaining concentration of impact on non-Bt smallholder maize is addressed by a two-season DALRRD grower-advisory + targeted subsidy on substitute inputs. Cost is bounded by the size of the non-Bt smallholder pool and the substitute-cost delta, and is small relative to the household-borne mortality cost retired by the ban (~R1.4–2.6B/yr at the working bracket).

6.5 6.5 Sensitivity

Three-factor sensitivity for the SA maize yield impact of a terbufos ban. Even the joint upper bound is well within the brief’s “<5 %” headline.
Driver Plausible range Effect on \(\Delta Y\)
Bt-trait coverage \(s_{\text{non-Bt}}\) 0.10 – 0.20 linear
Current terbufos use on non-Bt area \(u_{\text{terbufos}}\) 0.30 – 0.70 linear
Substitute yield gap \(\delta_{\text{substitute}}\) 0.00 – 0.10 linear
Combined upper bound — ~1.4 %
Combined working value — ~0.4 %

7 7. Limitations

  • \(\varphi_\text{retired}\) is the biggest single uncertainty. Substitution to other class I OPs would shrink this dramatically. Restricting the whole class (not just terbufos) is the obvious mitigation.
  • The model assumes the detection signal exists: without sentinel toxicology (Appendix D) and a working coordination loop (Appendix B), the regulatory action can’t fire on time.
  • No discounting applied — the 200–400/yr estimate is a steady-state annual figure.

#| label: setup
library(tidyverse)
library(gt)

raw <- readr::read_csv("amua_import_parameters_v3.csv", show_col_types = FALSE)
pick <- function(name) {
  v <- raw |> dplyr::filter(Name == name) |> dplyr::pull(Expression)
  if (length(v) == 0) NA_real_ else as.numeric(v[1])
}
zar <- function(x) {
  n <- suppressWarnings(as.numeric(x))
  if (is.na(n)) return(x)
  if (n >= 1e6) sprintf("R%.2f million", n / 1e6)
  else sprintf("R%s", format(round(n), big.mark = ",", scientific = FALSE))
}

f_lo  <- pick("frac_terbufos_attributable_lo")
f_mid <- pick("frac_terbufos_attributable_mid")
f_hi  <- pick("frac_terbufos_attributable_hi")

# 1. The formula

Annual deaths averted by removing terbufos from informal channels:

$$
\widehat{D}_{T2} \;=\; f_\text{attr} \times \varphi_\text{retired} \times N_\text{deaths}
$$

where

- $f_\text{attr}$ = terbufos attributable fraction of fatal SA pesticide poisonings (CSV: `frac_terbufos_attributable_{lo,mid,hi}` = `r f_lo`, `r f_mid`, `r f_hi`)
- $\varphi_\text{retired}$ = fraction of terbufos exposure successfully retired by enforcement (substitution-adjusted; placeholder 0.6 working, 0.4–0.8 sensitivity)
- $N_\text{deaths}$ = headline annual deaths (CSV: `n_deaths_headline` = `r format(N, big.mark = ",")`)

# 2. Bracketed estimate

```{r}
#| label: tbl-bracket
phi_grid <- c(lo = 0.4, mid = 0.6, hi = 0.8)

tibble::tibble(
  Scenario = c("Lower bound (conservative)", "Working value", "Upper bound (optimistic)"),
  `f_attr` = c(f_lo, f_mid, f_hi),
  `φ_retired` = c(0.4, 0.6, 0.8),
  `N_deaths` = N
) |>
  dplyr::mutate(
    `Deaths averted/yr` = round(`f_attr` * `φ_retired` * `N_deaths`)
  ) |>
  gt() |>
  fmt_percent(columns = c(`f_attr`, `φ_retired`), decimals = 0) |>
  fmt_number(columns = c(`N_deaths`, `Deaths averted/yr`), decimals = 0) |>
  tab_header(
    title = "Bracketed deaths averted per year, Option T2 (restriction + enforcement)",
    subtitle = "Working bracket cited in the Terbufos brief: ~200–400/yr"
  ) |>
  opt_row_striping()
```

Working value: $\widehat{D}_{T2}^{\text{mid}} = `r f_mid` \times 0.6 \times `r N` = `r round(f_mid * 0.6 * N)`$ deaths/yr.

Honest bracket: `r round(f_lo * 0.4 * N)` (lower) — `r round(f_hi * 0.8 * N)` (upper).

# 3. Cost side

```{r}

tibble::tibble(

```

# 4. Cost per death averted

Working value: cost/death-averted = `r zar(pick("c_T2_RUP_enforcement_mid") / round(f_mid * 0.6 * N))`.

This is well below any reasonable willingness-to-pay anchor for SA (e.g. 1× GDP/capita per DALY ≈ R85,000–R130,000), so **T2 dominates "do nothing" even at the lower bound of deaths averted.**
